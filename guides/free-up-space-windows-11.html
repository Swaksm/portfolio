Free up disk space on Windows 11 without breaking anything

Windows says your drive is almost full. Before deleting things at random, look at what takes the space, then clean in order: what is free and safe first, what has a cost last.

1. See what takes the space

Open Settings › System › Storage: Windows shows the space used by category (apps, temporary files, documents…). It is a good start, but it does not tell you which folder is heavy or whether you can delete it.

For that you need a scan that lists the largest files and folders. Look first at the top 20: on most PCs, a handful of folders explains most of the space.

2. Empty the caches that are safe to delete

These files are copies or leftovers that programs rebuild on their own. Deleting them frees space without losing anything:

  • Your temporary files: %TEMP%, that is C:\Users\<you>\AppData\Local\Temp.
  • Windows' temporary files: C:\Windows\Temp. Keep the files from the last 24 hours, an installer may still need them.
  • Browser caches (Chrome, Edge, Firefox…): only the cache, never cookies, passwords or history.
  • Updates already installed: C:\Windows\SoftwareDistribution\Download and the Delivery Optimization cache.
  • Crash reports and memory dumps, thumbnail cache, shader caches.
  • Developer caches if you code: npm, pip, NuGet, Gradle, Cargo.

3. The big items, each with a cost

These can free several gigabytes, but each one takes something away. Create a Windows restore point first, and only do the ones whose cost you accept:

  • Windows.old, $WINDOWS.~BT, $WINDOWS.~WS: the previous version of Windows after a major update. Cost: you can no longer roll back. Windows deletes them by itself after about 10 days.
  • Old components in C:\Windows\WinSxS, through Windows' own command Dism.exe /Online /Cleanup-Image /StartComponentCleanup. Cost: installed updates can no longer be uninstalled.
  • hiberfil.sys, the hibernation file, often several gigabytes: powercfg /hibernate off. Cost: no more hibernation or fast startup.
  • Old restore points, keeping the most recent one. Cost: you cannot go back further than the latest point.
  • CompactOS (compact /CompactOS:always): compresses Windows itself, about 2 to 5 GB. Cost: can be slightly slower on an old PC.

4. Your own files: only you can decide

  • Installers left in Downloads for months.
  • Archives you already extracted, duplicate photos and videos.
  • Games you no longer play (uninstall them from their launcher).
  • Old code projects: node_modules, .venv, target folders can be rebuilt with one command.
  • Send them to the Recycle Bin rather than deleting them for good, and empty it once you are sure.

What not to do

  • Delete things by hand in C:\Windows, WinSxS or Program Files: it breaks updates or programs.
  • Use a registry cleaner: it frees nothing useful, and Microsoft does not support them.
  • Delete pagefile.sys: Windows manages it, and the fix is in the virtual memory settings.
  • Run an « optimizer » that ticks everything in advance without telling you what each item is.

Questions

Is it safe to delete temporary files on Windows 11?

Yes for files that are not in use: Windows and programs recreate what they need. Files in use are locked and simply skipped. To be extra safe, only delete those older than 24 hours.

Why is my C: drive full when I have not installed anything?

Updates, caches and the hibernation file grow on their own. After a major Windows update, the previous version (Windows.old) can also take tens of gigabytes for about 10 days.

How much free space does Windows 11 need?

Microsoft asks for 64 GB of storage to install Windows 11. Day to day, keep at least 10 to 15% of the drive free so updates install and the PC stays fast.
